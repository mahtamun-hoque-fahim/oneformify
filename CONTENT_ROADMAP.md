# Formify — 30-Day Post-Launch Content Roadmap

> Written by Airborne on 2026-07-26. This is a publication plan, not a task list.
> Every item below has a URL slug, target keyword, word count target, and a content brief.
> When you are ready to write a piece, open its section, follow the brief, and publish.
> Publish order matters — cornerstone first, then cluster articles, then comparison pages.

---

## How to Use This File

1. Work top to bottom. Cornerstone pieces go live first because cluster articles link back to them.
2. Every piece has a STATUS field. Change it as you work: `PLANNED → WRITING → REVIEW → LIVE`.
3. When a piece goes live, update the URL column with the real published URL.
4. Do not publish everything at once. Spread over 30 days — 3–4 pieces per week signals freshness to Google without burning yourself out.

### 1. Google Forms Alternative Cornerstone

```
STATUS:       PLANNED
SLUG:         /blog/google-forms-alternative
TITLE:        Why Google Forms Is Not Enough — And What to Use Instead
META TITLE:   Google Forms Alternative That Actually Looks Good | Formify (57 chars)
META DESC:    Google Forms works but it is ugly, passive, and easy to cheat. Here is a complete comparison of the best alternatives — and why Formify is the one worth switching to. (172 chars)
H1:           The honest guide to Google Forms alternatives in 2026
PRIMARY KW:   google forms alternative (est. 40–60K/mo)
SECONDARY KW: better than google forms, google forms vs typeform, free form builder
SCHEMA:       Article + FAQPage
WORD COUNT:   2,000–2,500
INTERNAL LINKS TO: /, /features/anti-cheat-quiz, /blog/typeform-vs-formify
```

**Content brief:**

Open with the problem: Google Forms is free, fast to set up, and works — but it looks like 2012, has no visual progress feedback, zero quiz integrity, and analytics that amount to a pie chart. Then do a three-column comparison table: Google Forms vs Typeform vs Formify across 10 criteria (design, quiz mode, anti-cheat, analytics, export, price, conditional logic, progress bar, custom thank-you, mobile). Formify wins on design, anti-cheat, and price. Typeform wins on polish. Google Forms wins on nothing except being already-installed.

Sections to write:
- What Google Forms does well (1 paragraph — be honest, do not trash it cheaply)
- Where Google Forms falls short (design, quiz integrity, analytics — 3 subheadings)
- The comparison table (12 rows, honest ratings)
- Why Formify exists (1–2 paragraphs, personal voice)
- How to switch from Google Forms to Formify (numbered steps, link to signup)
- FAQ (5 questions — "Is Formify free?", "Does Formify work on mobile?", "Can I import my Google Forms?", "Does Formify prevent cheating?", "How do I share a Formify form?")

Do not pad. Do not write filler. Each section should answer a real question a switcher would have.

```
META TITLE:   Anti-Cheat Quiz Maker with Tab Detection | Formify (52 chars)

H1:           The quiz maker that knows when you switch tabs
PRIMARY KW:   anti cheat quiz maker (est. 5–10K/mo, low competition)
SECONDARY KW: quiz with tab detection, online quiz cheating prevention, focus monitoring quiz
SCHEMA:       FAQPage + Product
WORD COUNT:   800–1,200
INTERNAL LINKS TO: /, /blog/how-to-prevent-cheating, /signup
```

**Content brief:**

This is a feature landing page, not a blog post. Short, punchy, benefit-led. Open with the problem in one sentence: "Respondents switch tabs, use AI, and look up answers — and Google Forms cannot tell." Explain how Formify's focus monitoring works in plain language: the Page Visibility API detects when the quiz taker leaves the tab, a configurable three-strike model triggers warn → flag → auto-submit. No browser extensions, no software installs, works on any device.

Sections:
- How it works (3 bullet points, not paragraphs)
- Who it is for (teachers, HR teams, certification bodies, course creators)

- Three-strike model explained (with a simple diagram if possible)
- FAQ (4 questions: "Can students bypass it?", "Does it record the screen?", "Can I turn it off?", "What counts as a violation?")
- CTA to create a quiz

Be honest: tab detection is deterrence, not enforcement. A determined cheater can still cheat on a different device. Frame it as a friction layer that stops casual cheating and documents intent — which is what teachers and HR teams actually need.

```

H1:           7 ways to prevent cheating in online quizzes
PRIMARY KW:   how to prevent cheating in online quizzes (est. 8–15K/mo)
SECONDARY KW: online quiz cheating prevention, prevent tab switching quiz, quiz integrity
SCHEMA:       Article + FAQPage
WORD COUNT:   1,500–2,000
INTERNAL LINKS TO: /features/anti-cheat-quiz, /blog/how-to-create-online-quiz, /signup
```

**Content brief:**

Educational roundup. Not a product pitch — a genuinely useful guide that Formify happens to solve parts of. Frame each method honestly: some are technical (tab detection, timer limits), some are pedagogical (open-book by design, unique question banks), some are administrative (honour pledge, randomised question order).

7 methods:
1. Tab-switch detection (what it catches, what it misses)
2. Time limits (pressure reduces lookup time)
3. Question randomisation (different order per respondent)
4. Unique question banks (rotate questions per session)
5. Open-book by design (design questions that require thinking, not recall)
6. Honour pledge at the start (psychologically effective, research-backed)
7. Violation logging + manual review (documentation of intent)

For methods 1 and 7, mention Formify naturally — those are what Formify actually does. For others, be neutral. Link to the anti-cheat feature page at the end.

```

H1:           Typeform vs Formify vs Google Forms — the 2026 comparison
PRIMARY KW:   typeform vs google forms (est. 8–12K/mo)
SECONDARY KW: typeform alternative free, formify vs typeform, best form builder 2026
SCHEMA:       Article + Table (via schema.org/Table is not supported — use Article)
WORD COUNT:   1,500–2,000
INTERNAL LINKS TO: /blog/google-forms-alternative, /, /signup
```

**Content brief:**

Three-way comparison. Be fair. Typeform is genuinely better at the one-question-at-a-time conversational flow — acknowledge that. Google Forms is genuinely easy and deeply integrated with Google Workspace — acknowledge that. Formify wins on price (free vs Typeform's $25/mo), anti-cheat (unique feature), and dark-mode design (nobody else has it).

Structure:
- 1-paragraph summary of each tool
- Comparison table: 12 criteria, three columns
- Best for: section (Typeform = marketing surveys, Google Forms = internal quick polls, Formify = quizzes + premium-looking forms on a budget)
- Pricing breakdown (be factual: Typeform Basic $25/mo, Google Forms free, Formify free)
- Verdict

Do not fake any scores. Do not give Formify a 10/10 on everything. A biased comparison is obvious to readers and destroys trust.
